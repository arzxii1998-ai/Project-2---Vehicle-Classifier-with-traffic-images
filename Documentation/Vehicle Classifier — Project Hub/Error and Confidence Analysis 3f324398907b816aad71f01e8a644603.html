Error and Confidence Analysis

Despite nearly identical error patterns, CE and BCE tell very different stories through their loss and confidence values—showing why accuracy alone cannot explain how these models fail.

Setup. We analyze the validation errors of the two best ResNet18 models, which share the same recipe and differ only in the loss: resnet18_full (CE, 97.41% accuracy, val loss 0.1152) and resnet18_full_bce (BCE, 97.67%, val loss 0.029). Each error table lists every misclassified validation image with its true class, predicted class, and confidence: 20 errors for CE and 18 for BCE (751/771 and 753/771 correct). The analysis was triggered by an observation across the project's runs: validation loss does not follow the accuracy order, even within the same loss family. For example, resnet18_full has a higher accuracy than resnet18_finetune (97.41% vs. 97.02%) but also a higher loss (0.115 vs. 0.105).

1. Validation loss is dominated by a few confident errors. The loss is a mean over images, and a CE error costs −ln(p_true), which grows without bound as the probability of the true class approaches zero. Since in misclassified cases we have: p_true ≤ 1 − confidence, each error costs at least −ln(1 − confidence). Using this bound, the 20 CE errors account for at least 81% of the total validation loss (0.1152 × 771 ≈ 88.8, this is roughly the total val_loss), the four most confident errors for at least 32%, and the single worst one (image 227, confidence 0.99998) for at least 12%. A model with fewer errors can therefore have a higher loss than a model with more errors, if its errors are more confident. Loss measures how badly the worst few predictions fail, not how often the model is right.

2. CE and BCE losses are not comparable. BCE averages over 8 binary outputs, 7 of which are easy negatives, so the same mistake costs several times less than under CE. Image 227 was misclassified with confidence of about 0.9999 by both models: its CE cost is at least 10.7, while its BCE cost is only about 1 to 2, hence the BCE averages the loss on the 8 classes while CE don’t.ہ The gap in validation loss (0.1152 vs. 0.029) is therefore mostly a scale effect. Confidence values are also not on the same scale: softmax scores sum to one, whereas sigmoid scores are independent. Accuracy and F1 are the fair comparison between the two losses.

CE has more confident errors at 0.8, the two are equal at 0.9, and BCE has more at 0.99. On the 14 images that both models misclassify, BCE is more confident on 7 and less confident on 7. The gap at 0.8 comes mainly from three images (4, 370, and 566) where BCE lowered its confidence to 0.34 to 0.55.

4. Both models make the same mistakes. Fourteen errors are shared, 6 are CE-only (222, 240, 252, 258, 324, 741), and 4 are BCE-only (164, 177, 208, 746). The models disagree on only 10 images with a net difference of 2, and an exact McNemar test gives p ≈ 0.75, so there is no evidence that the loss function changes which images are misclassified beyond chance. This supports the caveat of the CE vs. BCE report.

Eight images are misclassified with confidence above 0.95 by both models (186, 199, 204, 217, 227, 254, 691, 695). A confident error on the same image by two differently trained models is typical of a label error or an inherently ambiguous image rather than of model overconfidence. These images should be inspected manually: if some labels are wrong, the reported accuracy underestimates the true one, and these images drive most of the validation loss.

The kamyun/kamyunet pair accounts for 12 of 20 CE errors and 10 of 18 BCE errors, and the dominant direction flips between the two models. The models therefore do not resolve this pair, they only choose which side to err on. The vanet → savari confusion is one-directional (no savari → vanet errors) and appears in both models.

6. Where BCE behaves differently: low-confidence errors. Independent sigmoid scores allow BCE to rate all classes low, as for the two ambulance errors (0.36 and 0.34), whereas a softmax top score is at least 1/8 and was 0.60 and 0.85 for the same images under CE. With a review threshold of 0.70, BCE would send 5 of its 18 errors to a human and CE only 2 of its 20. The cost of this behaviour, namely the correct predictions that would also be flagged, cannot be read from these tables and should be measured with selective_metrics on the validation set for both models. The production model remains CE, whose softmax probabilities sum to one.

Caveats. One seed and one validation set of 771 images. The loss shares in section 1 are lower bounds and assume the error table and the logged validation loss come from the same checkpoint. The suspected label-noise images have not yet been inspected.
