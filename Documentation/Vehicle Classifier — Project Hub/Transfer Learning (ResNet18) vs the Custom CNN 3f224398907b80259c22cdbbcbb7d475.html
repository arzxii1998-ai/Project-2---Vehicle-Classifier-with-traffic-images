Transfer Learning (ResNet18) vs. the Custom CNN

Setup. All numbers are validation results (771 images, so one image ≈ 0.13 pp), with the best checkpoint chosen by macro-F1 and a single seed (42). The best non-pretrained model is full_ablation_pro, a 5-block CNN (channels 32→256, 0.98M parameters) with BCE loss, weight decay 0.01, ReduceLROnPlateau, dropout 0.3, and augmentation. It is a combined configuration, not a one-factor ablation. The ResNet18 runs are:

  • Frozen: the ImageNet backbone is frozen and only the 8-class linear head is trained (4,104 parameters).
  • Fine-tune: layer4 and the head are trained (8.40M parameters), warm-started from the frozen run's best checkpoint.
  • Full: all layers are trained (11.18M parameters), with CE or BCE loss.

For the fine-tune and full runs, the backbone LR is 1e-4 and the head LR is 1e-3.

Findings.

  1. Frozen ImageNet features alone were not sufficient. The frozen ResNet18 is 3.50 pp (27 images) below the custom CNN in accuracy and 3.73 pp in macro-F1. A single linear layer on generic ImageNet features did not separate these vehicle classes as well as a small CNN trained end to end on this data.
  1. Most of the gain came from adapting the deepest block. Unfreezing layer4 raised accuracy by 5.06 pp (39 images) over the frozen model. Training all layers added only 0.39 pp accuracy (3 images) over fine-tuning, though macro-F1 (+0.65 pp) and macro-recall (+1.27 pp) were higher. This suggests the early generic layers can stay pretrained, with a possible small extra benefit for some classes that we did not investigate.
  1. Pretraining gave a clear but moderate advantage under a similar recipe. resnet18_full_bce and full_ablation_pro share the loss, optimizer, weight decay, scheduler, dropout, augmentation, and epoch budget. The ResNet is 2.21 pp better in accuracy (17 images) and 2.32 pp in macro-F1, with 11.4× more trainable parameters. The CNN therefore comes within about 2 pp of the best pretrained model. The recipes differ in the backbone LR (see setup), so the gap cannot be attributed to pretraining alone.
  1. Pretrained models converged much faster. The full and fine-tune ResNet runs reached their best epoch at 13 to 23, while the custom CNN reached it at epoch 48 of 50, which suggests it had not fully converged and its true ceiling may be slightly higher.
  1. kamyun remains the weakest class even for the best models. It has the lowest precision in all four ResNet runs and the lowest recall in three of four (kamyunet in the CE full run). The residual error of 2.3 to 3.0% (about 18 to 23 images) therefore appears tied to the kamyun/kamyunet similarity rather than to model capacity. This is consistent with the confusion-matrix analysis, but it needs to be confirmed by inspecting the misclassified images.

Caveats. Each result is from a single seed, so differences of about 3 images or fewer (e.g., fine-tune vs. full) are within run-to-run noise. The fine-tune run is warm-started from the frozen run, so it has seen additional epochs, which makes the frozen-vs-fine-tune comparison not strictly controlled.
